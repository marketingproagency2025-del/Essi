By the First Cold Week, Your Year Is Already Decided.

Selling stoves, fires and heating is not like selling most things, because demand does not arrive steadily. It arrives all at once, triggered by weather nobody controls, and then it disappears again for eight months.

That single fact should shape almost every decision. Most heating businesses market hardest in the weeks when the phone is already ringing, which is the one period when extra demand is worth the least, and go quiet in the months when the next winter is actually being decided.

The Short Answer

Do the marketing in the quiet season and take the orders in the cold one. By the first genuinely cold week, the people who were going to plan ahead have already chosen somebody.

The reason is capacity rather than demand. When it turns cold everyone in your trade is busy at once, lead times stretch, and a customer ringing in November is choosing between whoever can actually fit before Christmas. If you are already full, more enquiries are worth nothing.

So the useful goal in summer is not orders. It is a list of people who have decided on you and are waiting for a date.

Demand Arrives All at Once, Then Disappears

A heating year is not a curve, it is a cliff. Interest is flat for months, then a cold snap arrives and the volume multiplies inside a week, and by the time you have reacted the spike is halfway through.

Campaigns cannot be switched on into that. Paid platforms need a stretch of spending before they work out who responds, and a campaign started on the first cold Monday spends its most expensive fortnight learning, at exactly the moment every competitor is bidding on the same people.

A campaign that has been running quietly since summer walks into that week already trained, with an audience that has seen you before. That is the whole argument for the quiet season, and it is a mechanical one rather than a matter of taste.

The Search Happens Late, on a Phone, in a Cold Room

The moment somebody starts looking is rarely convenient. It is the evening, the heating has failed or the room has never been warm enough, and they are on a phone in the room they are annoyed about.

That tells you what the page they land on has to do. Load quickly on a bad connection, say what you install and where, and make contacting you a single obvious action. Anything that needs a desktop, a long scroll or a form with nine fields loses to whoever made it easier.

It also tells you what to have written already. Somebody in that state is comparing wood against pellet, working out whether their flue is usable, and wondering what a room like theirs actually needs. Pages that answer those get found at the exact moment the question is being asked.

What to Run in the Quiet Season

Film the installs you are doing now. Summer is when the calm, well-lit, unhurried fittings happen, and that is the footage you will want in October when every day is a rush. A business that films in November films badly or not at all.

Sell the service rather than the appliance. Servicing, sweeping, flue checks and pre-winter safety are genuinely useful in August, they earn revenue in a flat month, and they put you in the house of somebody who will be thinking about replacing an old unit within a year or two.

And run the offer that suits the season honestly: fitted before the cold, at a date you choose, by people who are not currently rushed. That is a real advantage of buying early and it does not require discounting anything.

Capacity Is the Real Ceiling, Not Leads

Most trades can absorb more customers. This one cannot, at least not in the twelve weeks that matter, and that changes what good marketing looks like.

Once the diary is full, the job of the advertising switches from generating enquiries to protecting the ones you already have and filling the following spring. Spending harder into a full diary produces annoyed people who wanted a stove in three weeks and were told six.

Which is why the number to watch through the winter is not enquiries or cost per lead. It is how far out you are booking, and whether the people waiting are still with you when the date arrives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should we really advertise heating in the summer?

Yes, though not with the same message. Summer advertising is for servicing, flue checks and getting a fitting date booked before the rush, and it lets the campaign do its learning while bidding is cheap. It is not about persuading somebody to want heat in August; it is about being the firm they have already seen when the weather turns.

Our diary is full every winter anyway. Why bother?

Because a full diary in December says nothing about how full it was in October or will be in March, and because being full on price-insensitive work is different from being full on whatever came in. Marketing in the quiet months lets you fill the shoulders of the season and choose better jobs rather than taking what arrives.

Wood or pellet, which should we push?

Whichever you fit best, and say so plainly. This is one of the genuinely useful things to write about, because customers are actively comparing the two and most of what they find is written by manufacturers. A straight comparison from an installer who fits both, including the running and the cleaning, gets found and gets trusted.
