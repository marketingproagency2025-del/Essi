29 August 2026 · 6 min read · By Essi Papajorgji

One Buys You Applause. The Other Buys You a Phone Number.

The Boost button sits under every post you publish, it costs as little as you want it to, and it takes about four taps. The campaign sits behind Ads Manager, asks a dozen questions before it will spend anything, and most business owners never open it twice. Given that choice, almost everybody boosts.

That is not a mistake exactly. Boosting does something real. But it does a different thing from a campaign, and the difference is not budget or effort: it is what you are left holding at the end. One leaves you with a bigger number under a post. The other leaves you with people you can call.

The Short Answer

Boost a post when something you have already published is working and you want more of the right people to see it. Run a campaign when you want enquiries.

The Boost button cannot collect a contact detail, cannot show a different message to someone who has already visited your site, and cannot tell you which version of an ad produced a customer. If any of those three matter to you, you are not looking for a boost.

The quickest test is to ask what you would do with the result. If the answer is look at it, boost. If the answer is call them, run a campaign.

What the Boost Button Actually Does

Boosting is a campaign with almost every decision already made for you. Meta picks the objective, and it picks engagement or reach, because those are the outcomes it can reliably produce from a post that was written to be read rather than to be answered.

That single choice then propagates through everything else. Meta optimises towards whatever you asked it for, and it is extremely good at this. Ask for engagement and it will go and find the people most likely to tap a heart. That is a real audience. It is not the same audience as the people likely to come and buy a stove.

So the result is honest and slightly useless. The post did get seen by more people, the numbers underneath it did go up, and nothing at all entered your diary.

What a Campaign Does That a Boost Cannot

It lets you choose the objective. Leads, messages, calls, a form, a visit to the shop. The optimisation follows the objective, so you are asking the platform for the thing you actually want rather than the thing it defaults to.

It gives you a structure. Separate audiences, separate creatives, budgets you can move between them, and the ability to switch off the half that is not working without switching off the half that is.

It gives you retargeting. The people who watched most of your video, opened your site, or started your form and stopped are the warmest audience you will ever have. A boost has no way of reaching any of them a second time.

And it gives you measurement that reaches past the post itself. A boost tells you how many people saw it. A campaign tells you what a lead cost, which ad produced it, and whether that lead ever turned into a customer.

The Gap, in Numbers We Can Show

Across seven Meta campaigns we reached 1,084,529 people and generated 6,150 leads, at a blended cost of 1.87 euro per lead, with individual campaigns landing between 1.47 and 2.30 euro. Total spend was 11,480.98 euro.

The figure that matters in that paragraph is not the cost. It is that there are 6,150 rows with a name and a number in them. A boost, at any budget at all, produces none of those, because it never asks anybody for one.

That is the whole difference restated as arithmetic. Same platform, same money, a different thing to show for it at the end of the month.

When Boosting Is Genuinely the Right Call

There are real cases, and pretending otherwise would make this post advertising rather than advice.

A post that is already performing on its own. If people are saving and sharing something without being asked to, putting money behind it is buying more of a thing that is already proven rather than gambling on a new one.

Local awareness on a small budget. If the goal is simply that people within a few kilometres know the showroom is there, then reach is the outcome, and reach is exactly what the button delivers.

An event, a sale window or an opening. Short lived, tied to a date, and not worth building a structure around.

What all of those have in common is that awareness is the outcome, not a step towards one. The moment the outcome you want is a conversation, the button runs out of road.

The Part That Decides Whether Either One Works

Neither a boost nor a campaign closes anything. Both of them hand you attention, and attention goes cold quickly.

The 6,150 leads above were all called and qualified before they reached the sales teams they belonged to. That is not an extra service bolted onto the advertising. It is the reason the advertising has a number attached to it at all, because a lead that nobody rings is indistinguishable from a lead that never arrived.

The Short Version

  • Boosting optimises for engagement, so it finds people who react rather than people who buy.
  • A campaign lets you choose the objective, collect a contact detail, retarget and measure through to the enquiry.
  • Boost when awareness really is the outcome: a proven organic post, a local radius, a dated event.
  • Across seven campaigns we produced 6,150 leads at a blended 1.87 euro. A boost produces none, because it never asks for a contact.
  • Neither one closes. What decides the result is who calls the lead, and how quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is boosting a post a waste of money?

No, but it is easy to spend it on the wrong outcome. Boosting reliably buys reach and engagement, and if that is what you need it is the cheapest way to get it. It becomes a waste when it is used as a substitute for a campaign, because no amount of boosting will produce a contact you can call.

Can I run a proper campaign on a small budget?

Yes. Budget changes how fast you learn, not whether the structure works. A small daily budget on one clear objective with one audience will still collect contact details and still tell you what a lead cost, which a boost of the same size will not. What a small budget cannot do is test many variations at once, so start with fewer of them.

How soon will a campaign produce enquiries?

Usually within days of going live, although the first stretch is partly the platform working out who responds. We report properly at 30 days, because that is the first point at which the numbers mean something rather than describing the learning period.
