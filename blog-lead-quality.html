The Lead Was Not Fake. It Went Cold While You Were Busy.

It is one of the most common complaints in advertising, and it always arrives in the same shape. The campaign worked. The leads came in, more of them than expected, and cheaply. Then somebody sat down to ring through the list and half of them did not pick up, a quarter did not remember filling anything in, and the rest wanted something the business does not sell.

The usual conclusion is that the leads were fake. They almost never are. What actually happened is that three separate things were set up to produce exactly this result, and all three of them are inside your control rather than the platform's.

The Short Answer

Unanswered leads have three causes, and most campaigns are running at least two of them at once. The campaign was optimised for the cheapest possible form fill. The form was easy enough to complete by accident. And nobody called for two days.

None of those is Meta misbehaving. Each one is an instruction the platform was given and followed exactly. Change the instruction and the same budget produces a different kind of person on the other end of the phone.

If you only fix one, fix the third. Speed is the lever with the shortest distance between changing it and seeing the result.

Cheap Leads Are a Target You Set

Ad platforms optimise for whatever you asked for, with no opinion about whether it was a sensible thing to ask for. Tell it to minimise cost per lead and it will find you the people who submit forms most readily, which is a real and identifiable group, and not the same group as people ready to spend money.

This is why a falling cost per lead can be a warning rather than a win. If the number keeps dropping while the diary stays empty, the campaign is getting better at the thing you measured and worse at the thing you wanted.

The fix is to move the target further down the line. Optimise for a qualified enquiry, a booked call or a purchase rather than a submission, and accept that the cost per lead will rise. It is supposed to. You are paying more for fewer people who are worth ringing.

An Instant Form Is One Tap Away From an Accident

Meta instant forms pre-fill the name, email and phone number from the profile. That is the entire point of them and it is why they are cheap. It also means a person can complete one while half watching something else, out of mild curiosity, without ever forming the intention to be contacted.

A form that takes no thought to complete selects for people who did not think about it. When they get a call two days later, they are not being evasive. They genuinely do not remember, because there was nothing to remember.

The cure is friction, applied deliberately and in small amounts. One question that only a real prospect can answer, asked in your own words, will cut the volume and raise the answer rate. Which product are you looking at. When are you hoping to have it installed. What is the room. None of those are hard for a buyer and all of them stop an idle tap.

The Promise in the Ad Decides Who Replies

An ad is a filter before it is anything else. Whatever it promises is what people are responding to, so if it leads with a discount, a giveaway or a vague offer of information, the people who answer are the ones who wanted a discount, a giveaway or some information.

They are not bad leads for having believed the ad. They are the leads the ad asked for.

Ads that name the product, the price bracket or the commitment will always produce fewer responses. That is the mechanism working. The people filtered out by an honest ad were going to be filtered out later by a phone call, and it is considerably cheaper to lose them at the first step.

Speed Is the Biggest Single Lever

A person who fills in a form is, at that exact moment, thinking about your product. Ten minutes later they are thinking about something else, and by the following afternoon they have forgotten and possibly enquired somewhere else as well.

The gap between the form landing and the phone ringing does more damage to lead quality than targeting, creative and budget combined, and it is the only one of the four that costs nothing to change.

It is also why leads feel worse on weekends, over holidays and during busy weeks. Nothing changed about the advertising. What changed is that the list sat there.

How to Tell a Lead Problem From a Follow-Up Problem

There is one question that separates them, and most businesses cannot answer it: how long, on average, between a form arriving and the first call being made.

If nobody knows, it is a follow-up problem, and no amount of retargeting or new creative will touch it. Measure that number for a fortnight before changing anything in the ad account.

If the answer is genuinely minutes, and the people picking up still want something you do not sell, then it is a targeting and messaging problem, and the sections above are where to start.

The Short Version

  • Unanswered leads are rarely fake. They are the predictable output of three settings you control.
  • Optimising for the cheapest form fill buys people who fill in forms, not people who buy.
  • Pre-filled instant forms can be completed without intent. One real qualifying question fixes most of it.
  • Whatever the ad promises is what the respondent wanted. Name the product rather than a discount.
  • Speed beats everything. If you cannot say how long from form to first call, that is the problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Facebook and Instagram leads just fake?

Very rarely. Bot submissions exist but they are a small fraction, and they look obviously wrong when you check the entries. What is far more common is a real person who tapped a pre-filled form without much intent and was contacted days later. Both of those problems are yours to fix rather than the platform's.

How quickly should a lead be called?

Within minutes if you can manage it, and the same working day at the outside. The person is thinking about your product at the moment they submit and about something else very shortly afterwards. If calling that fast is not realistic in-house, that is a strong argument for someone doing it on your behalf rather than for spending less on ads.

Will fixing this make my leads more expensive?

Yes, and that is the intended result. Qualifying earlier and optimising for a real enquiry removes the cheapest respondents, so the cost per lead rises. The number that should fall is the cost per customer, which is the only one that was ever worth watching.
